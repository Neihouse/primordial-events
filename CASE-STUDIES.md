# Event Case Studies - Primordial Events

## Purpose of Case Studies

Case studies serve multiple critical functions for Primordial Events:

- **Build Portfolio Credibility**: Demonstrate real-world expertise and successful event execution
- **Generate Testimonials**: Capture client feedback for marketing and reputation building
- **Create Marketing Content**: Develop authentic stories for website, social media, and proposals
- **Improve Future Planning**: Learn from each event to refine processes and packages
- **Track What Works**: Document equipment performance, setup efficiency, and timing optimization
- **Support Sales**: Provide similar event examples when proposing to new clients
- **Venue Partnerships**: Show venue coordinators our professional execution

### Challenge 1: Outdoor Ceremony Wind Noise
- **Issue**: Unexpected 15mph winds during ceremony threatened audio quality
- **Impact**: Lapel mics picking up significant wind noise, distorting vows
- **Solution**: Switched to directional handheld mics for vows, repositioned speakers to create "audio pocket", increased volume strategically
- **Outcome**: Crystal clear audio for all 150 guests, multiple compliments on ceremony sound

### Challenge 2: Venue Power Limitations
- **Issue**: Venue's outdoor power only had 2x 15A circuits available for ceremony
- **Impact**: Not enough power for full ceremony sound system + backup power concerns
- **Solution**: Deployed generator backup (brought proactively), ran ceremony speakers on battery power, kept generator as hot spare
- **Outcome**: No power issues, seamless ceremony audio

### Challenge 3: Last-Minute Song Changes
- **Issue**: Bride changed processional song 30 minutes before ceremony
- **Impact**: New song not in prepared library
- **Solution**: Downloaded from Beatport Mobile while setting up ceremony speakers, tested during sound check
- **Outcome**: Perfect execution, bride never knew it was a scramble

- **Power**: Limited outdoor power (only 2x 15A circuits) - required generator backup
- **Space**: Reception ballroom had low ceiling (8ft) - adjusted moving head placement
- **Sound Restrictions**: Outdoor ceremony had 85dB limit until 6pm, indoor reception 95dB limit, hard stop at 11pm

## Lessons Learned

### What Worked Exceptionally Well
1. **Proactive generator backup**: Bringing generator "just in case" saved the ceremony when venue power proved inadequate
2. **Separate ceremony sound system**: Battery-powered wireless speakers allowed complete independence from venue power and quick transition
3. **Pre-event venue visit (1 week prior)**: Walking the space identified wind exposure and power limitations early
4. **Bilingual music expertise**: Deep Latin music library and mixing skills were key differentiator for this client

### What to Improve Next Time
1. **Bring wind protection for mics**: Invest in better foam windscreens for outdoor ceremonies
2. **Extended cable management**: 200ft runs were messy - need better cable ramps and organization for long distances
3. **Heat management**: 88°F outdoor temp stressed amplifiers - bring portable fan for equipment in hot outdoor setups
4. **Timeline buffer**: Ceremony started 15 min late (normal for weddings) - build in more buffer time for outdoor events

### Equipment Performance Notes
- **Performed Well**: QSC CP8 battery-powered speakers were clutch for ceremony (6hr battery life, used 2hrs), moving heads created amazing ambiance, ULXD wireless mics had zero dropouts
- **Issues Encountered**: One lavalier transmitter had brief interference (possibly from venue WiFi), quickly swapped to backup
- **Maintenance Needed**: Clean dust from moving heads (outdoor use), check battery levels on CP8s
- **Replacement Consideration**: Consider upgrading to premium foam windscreens for all wireless mics (outdoor performance)

### Timing Adjustments
- Load-in time was adequate but tight (3.5hrs for dual setup locations) - recommend 4hrs for future outdoor ceremony + indoor reception combos
- Ceremony-to-reception transition was smooth (35 min cocktail hour gave plenty of time)
- Load-out took longer than expected (1.5hrs) due to long distance to parking - factor this in for future vineyard/outdoor venues

### Client Communication Insights
- Weekly planning calls (4 total) prevented surprises and built trust
- Couple was very organized with song lists and timeline - made execution smooth
- Last-minute changes (song swap) were communicated with only 30min notice - need to set expectations about "change cutoff time" in future contracts
- Bilingual communication (some calls in Spanish with groom's family) built strong rapport

### Challenge 1: Sound Balance in Brewery

- **Solution**: Repositioned speakers 15 degrees inward (narrower coverage), reduced bass to minimize low-end buildup, used EQ to cut mid-range feedback frequencies
- **Outcome**: Crystal clear speech, no feedback during entire event

### Challenge 2: Trivia Software Integration
- **Issue**: Venue's Windows laptop (Sporcle Live software) had HDMI output but no audio output jack, only built-in speakers
- **Impact**: Couldn't route trivia software audio (music round clips) through main PA initially
- **Solution**: Used Focusrite Scarlett interface with USB input from laptop, routed trivia audio to main PA via mixer
- **Outcome**: Seamless integration, music round clips played perfectly through PA

### Challenge 3: First-Time Trivia Hosting
- **Issue**: Venue had never hosted trivia night before, staff unsure of logistics (prize distribution, food service timing)
- **Impact**: Some confusion during first round about when to serve food
- **Solution**: Quick huddle with venue manager before event, agreed on food service during rounds (not breaks), coordinated prize table setup
- **Outcome**: Smooth execution, venue staff appreciated guidance

### Venue Limitations
- **Power**: Adequate (3x 20A circuits available)
- **Space**: Tight setup area behind bar (8x8ft) - required compact booth layout
- **Sound Restrictions**: None (commercial venue, no residential neighbors)
- **Access**: Easy load-in through side door, 20ft to setup location
- **Other**: Ambient noise from bar/kitchen required louder PA volume than typical venue this size

### What Doesn't Require Permission (Generally)

- Equipment notes and maintenance tracking
- Timeline and logistics (for improving future events)

**Public use (usually OK):**
- Equipment photos (your own gear, no client branding)

**If serving EU clients or events in EU:**
- Include GDPR-compliant permission form (right to be forgotten)
- Store client data securely (encrypted, password-protected)
- Allow clients to request data deletion at any time
- Document consent and permission dates

**Best practices (even if not legally required):**
- Never share client contact info with third parties
- Store client data securely (password-protected files)
- Honor deletion requests promptly (remove from website within 7 days)
- Use anonymized data for internal analysis (remove names from spreadsheets)

### Permission Form Best Practices

**When to collect:**
- At contract signing (ideal - built into agreement)
- After event (via email with photos for approval)
- Before posting (send draft post for approval)

**How to make it easy:**
- Use simple language (no legal jargon)
- Include checkboxes (easy to skim and sign)
- Offer digital signature (DocuSign, HelloSign, or email confirmation)
- Provide examples (show them what you'll post)

**What to include:**
- Specific content types (photos, names, testimonials, video)
- Where it will be used (website, social media, print)
